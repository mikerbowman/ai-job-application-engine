# Cover Letters and Screening Answers

## Cover-letter structure

Most letters should be roughly 300–450 words.

1. Name the role and the part of its mandate that is genuinely compelling.
2. Give one recent, relevant proof story with scope, action, measurement, and outcome.
3. Add only the breadth needed to prove range, collaboration, or domain transfer.
4. Connect working style and judgment to something real about the employer.
5. State the value the candidate can bring and invite conversation.

Do not repeat the resume chronologically. Interpret the evidence and show how the candidate thinks.

## Optional fields

Treat an available cover-letter field as useful, even when labeled optional, if a tailored letter can add motivation, judgment, context, or a credible explanation of an adjacent background.

## Screening answers

- Answer the exact question in the first sentence.
- Explain the principle in the candidate's voice.
- Ground it in a concise example.
- End on relevance to the role.

## Final edit

Read aloud for cadence. Remove canned language, duplicated resume bullets, stacked abstractions, and sentences that could belong to any candidate. Verify the employer name, role, location, and product terminology.
